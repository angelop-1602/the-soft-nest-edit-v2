Kitchen drawers are valuable in a small apartment because they hide the tools you use every day without occupying counter space. Start with what you cook and how you reach for it. The right layout can be made from containers you already own.

## 1. Empty one drawer at a time

Choose the drawer you open most often. Remove its contents, clean it and sort tools into daily use, occasional use and duplicates. Keep sentimental or specialty tools only if you have a realistic home for them.

## 2. Measure the usable interior

Measure the inside width and depth, including any narrow sections near runners, and the clearance from the bottom to the next drawer. A divider that looks shallow enough may still catch when a utensil stands upright. Try a paper template before purchasing.

## 3. Group tools by task

Put everyday cutlery near the place you eat or unload dishes. Keep cooking utensils near preparation areas if the layout permits. Separate sharp tools in a suitable protective organizer rather than letting blades slide among loose objects.

## 4. Fit dividers to the drawer

The [Pipishell expandable bamboo organizer](/shop/pipishell-bamboo-drawer-organizer/) is an option for separating utensils. Compare its contracted and expanded dimensions with your measurements. A few small boxes can serve the same function; ensure they do not slide or jam the drawer.

## 5. Review the layout after a week

Notice which compartments become crowded and which stay empty. Move frequently used tools closer and put occasional items elsewhere. Wipe away crumbs, keep the tray dry and follow its care directions. The best drawer is one you can open, use and close without rearranging everything.

## Make a paper template first

Trace the usable drawer floor onto a sheet of paper, marking the narrowest point, any screws and the height available below the cabinet frame. Arrange your most-used utensils on the template before shopping. Give long tools their own uninterrupted slot, and check that tall handles will not catch when closing the drawer. Keep knives safely stored in a suitable protected holder rather than loose among forks. In a rental kitchen, removable inserts are easier to clean and move than glued-in dividers.
