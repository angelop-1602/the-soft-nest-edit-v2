A storage basket works when it has one clear purpose and sits where the objects are used. A basket that looks pretty but requires you to carry things across the room is likely to become a dumping ground. Use what you already own first, then choose sizes that suit the actual contents.

## 1. Keep a blanket beside the sofa

Fold or loosely roll the throw you use most often. A [woven blanket basket](/shop/oiahomy-woven-blanket-basket/) is one possible option if it fits beside the sofa without narrowing the walkway. Check the material and the usable interior size before buying.

## 2. Catch small entryway items

Use a small shallow basket on an existing shelf for gloves or scarves. Keep keys and wallets in a predictable separate spot. Avoid deep baskets where small items disappear under larger ones.

## 3. Collect dry spare towels

Place clean folded towels in a dry area of the home. Avoid storing damp laundry inside decorative woven baskets, and follow the basket’s cleaning instructions.

## 4. Group reading materials

Keep current books and magazines near the seat where you read. A sturdy low basket can make them easier to return. Avoid overfilling it with heavy books, particularly if it sits on a shelf.

## 5. Create a simple hobby station

Assign a basket to one hobby, such as knitting supplies, so you can lift it to the place where you work. Secure small or sharp items in suitable containers out of reach of children and pets.

## 6. Organize a shelf with categories

Use a basket for one kind of dry, lightweight item. If several identical baskets sit on a shelf, a small label or distinct texture can make the contents easy to identify. Measure the shelf and leave space to grip the basket.

## 7. Use one temporary reset basket

During a quick tidy, collect items that belong elsewhere and return them before the end of the session. Do not keep a permanent basket of unsorted clutter. The goal is fewer decisions when putting things away, not hiding the decisions indefinitely.
