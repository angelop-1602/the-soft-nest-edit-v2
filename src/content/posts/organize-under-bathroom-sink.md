The space under a bathroom sink is not a regular rectangular cabinet. Pipes, shutoff valves and door hinges can interrupt otherwise useful space. Build the storage plan around those features instead of choosing an organizer first.

## 1. Inspect the cabinet before filling it

Remove the contents and look for moisture, leaks or damage. Address problems before adding storage. Keep access to shutoff valves and plumbing; do not cover a leak-prone area with a container that makes it difficult to inspect.

## 2. Measure the usable zones

Measure each side of the drain and the height below the pipes. Note hinge positions and the clearance needed to pull out a drawer. Use a cardboard mockup or a box you already own to test whether an organizer can slide in and open without catching.

## 3. Assign zones by frequency

Put products you use most often in the reachable front zone. Reserve deeper space for dry backups. A [two-tier bathroom organizer](/shop/hihegd-two-tier-bathroom-organizer/) may suit one side of the plumbing if the selected version fits; check access and loading instructions.

## 4. Keep small accessories separate

A set of [clear drawers](/shop/vtopmart-clear-stackable-drawers/) can group dry hair accessories or spare toiletries on a stable shelf. Keep liquids in suitable upright containers, and do not stack drawers where pulling one open would destabilize the stack.

## 5. Handle cleaners and damp items carefully

Keep cleaning products in their original labeled containers and follow storage directions. Use child-resistant storage where appropriate. Do not store damp towels or wet sponges in a closed cabinet. Check the area periodically for leaks, remove unused items and leave the plumbing accessible.

## Sketch the plumbing footprint

Draw the cabinet from above and mark the sink trap, shutoff valves and any fixed shelves. Use empty cardboard boxes to test a possible organizer footprint, including the room needed to pull a drawer forward. Leave access to valves and check for leaks before storing anything. Store household cleaning products in their original containers according to their labels, secured away from children and pets. Reserve the driest area for toiletries and use a removable, washable liner only where it will not conceal a leak.
